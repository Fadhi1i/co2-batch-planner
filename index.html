HOW IT WORKS

From your inputs to a trial plan.

1. Calculate the materials

Total concrete mass is divided according to the entered cement and aggregate mass ratios. Water is included using the water-to-cement ratio.

2. Calculate the CO₂ requirement

The target percentage and cement mass determine the required added CO₂ mass. Dividing by the calculated mixing-water volume gives the required uptake concentration.

3. Check the tested conditions

The planner compares the required uptake with the mean results at the selected speed. An exact match uses a tested setting mean. Other estimates use adjacent tested flow settings.

4. Review the operating estimate

Interpolated settings are starting estimates, not separately tested operating points. If several intervals fit the target, the alternatives are shown for you to review.

What happens outside the tested range?

The planner still calculates the batch materials and required CO₂ concentration. It does not assign a flow or pH estimate outside the tested mean uptake range.

Changing the pump speed may provide another tested range. Changing a mix design or dose target remains a separate decision.

What happens when the target dose is zero?

Material quantities are calculated normally. No CO₂ addition is requested, and no carbonation pH target is assigned.

What the batch calculation includes
  • Fresh concrete mass excludes the mould.
  • Cement, fine aggregate and coarse aggregate are entered as mass ratios.
  • The calculated batch contains cement, aggregates and water.
  • SCMs, admixtures, aggregate moisture, absorption corrections and handling losses are not included.
  • All calculated mixing water is carbonated.
  • The selected added CO₂ concentration is assumed to be retained until the water enters the mix.
  • Delivered dissolved CO₂ is different from CO₂ mineralised in concrete.

Four speeds. Individual trial records.

The 30 Hz water assumption

Water mass was not independently recorded on the scale during the 30 Hz series. An 8 kg water collection is assumed for those trial calculations.

This affects the trial dissolved masses and dissolution efficiencies. The PHREEQC uptake concentration and recorded handheld pH remain the supplied values.

Your batch water is calculated from your own mix inputs. The planner does not substitute the assumed 8 kg trial collection for your batch water.

Water delivery and operating estimates

Some trial collections differed from their water setpoints. Those observations help explain the tested operating conditions, but do not establish a correction factor for another batch size.

The calculated mixing-water requirement still needs to be delivered into the batch. The planner does not automatically increase a system setpoint to compensate for trial shortfalls.

Reading pH and uptake estimates correctly

Added uptake comes from the supplied PHREEQC results. The displayed outlet pH comes from handheld readings at the supporting settings.

Mean pH is a descriptive average of recorded readings, not the pH of physically mixed samples. Between-setting pH estimates use linear interpolation.

The planner does not adjust these estimates for changes in inlet chemistry, temperature, pressure or equipment configuration.
